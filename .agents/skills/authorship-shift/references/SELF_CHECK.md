# Fidelity Self-Check

Use this checklist silently before returning an information-dense rewrite.

## Immutable details

Confirm every source item in these categories survived unchanged unless the user explicitly requested an edit:

- numbers and units;
- percentages and ratios;
- dates, times, durations, and ages;
- names and proper nouns;
- quotations;
- citations and source attribution;
- formulas, code identifiers, product names, legal terms, and technical labels.

## Claims

For each required claim, verify that the final text still communicates the same proposition.

Watch for accidental changes caused by:

- dropping a condition;
- changing who performed an action;
- changing the object of a comparison;
- converting a possibility into a fact;
- converting an estimate into a measured result;
- turning an example into a general rule;
- replacing “some” or “most” with “all.”

## Causality

Check causal direction explicitly.

Do not convert:

- trigger → contributing factor;
- contributing factor → sole cause;
- correlation → causation;
- sequence → causation;
- failure to observe → evidence of absence.

## Qualifications

Search the source for words and constructions such as:

- may, might, could;
- approximately, roughly, about;
- estimate, assume, expect;
- suggests, indicates, associated with;
- limited to, only, within;
- however, although, except;
- cannot determine;
- does not establish;
- base case / scenario / hypothesis.

Make sure the rewrite preserves their practical effect, even if wording changes.

## Unsupported additions

Remove anything that came from general knowledge or stylistic improvisation rather than the source unless the user explicitly asked for expansion or research.

Common accidental additions:

- invented motivations;
- new causal explanations;
- extra examples presented as facts;
- fabricated personal experience;
- unsupported certainty;
- new recommendations;
- assumed demographic or organizational details.

## Quality

After fidelity passes, check that the rewrite also improved or preserved:

- clarity;
- specificity;
- logical flow;
- concision where appropriate;
- readable sentence structure;
- paragraph function;
- voice consistency.

If fidelity and style conflict, fidelity wins.
