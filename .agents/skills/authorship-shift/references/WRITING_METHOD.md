# AuthorshipShift Writing Method

The goal is document-level naturalness without sacrificing intellectual content.

## 1. Preserve the information architecture before changing the prose

A rewrite is only successful if its factual and argumentative payload survives. Track required claims, numbers, names, dates, technical terms, causal direction, comparisons, caveats, citations, and conclusions before making stylistic changes.

Distinguish between:

- **immutable content** — facts, figures, quotations, citations, names, dates, formulas, technical labels;
- **semantic content** — claims, reasoning, causal relationships, conclusions;
- **epistemic content** — how certain or uncertain the source is;
- **presentation** — order, paragraphing, syntax, examples, emphasis, transitions, diction.

Transform presentation aggressively when useful. Transform the first three categories only when the user explicitly asks to change the substance.

## 2. Break template-level regularity, not grammar

Weak machine-assisted prose often becomes predictable because the same rhetorical unit repeats:

1. generic topic sentence;
2. two or three balanced supporting points;
3. transition phrase;
4. mini-summary;
5. repeat.

Do not correct this by injecting randomness, mistakes, slang, fake anecdotes, or gratuitous sentence fragments. Instead change the rhetorical organization.

A paragraph may:

- state a conclusion and then narrow it;
- explain a mechanism before naming the implication;
- open with evidence;
- handle an objection;
- make a distinction;
- compress several obvious steps into one sentence;
- spend disproportionate space on the genuinely difficult point.

The result should feel shaped by the idea rather than by a universal writing template.

## 3. Use structural operators selectively

### Claim first

Open with the most consequential proposition, then explain why it is true. Useful for analysis, business writing, arguments, and recommendations.

### Mechanism first

Explain what happens and why before stating the broader conclusion. Useful when the causal chain is more interesting or persuasive than the headline.

### Evidence then judgment

Let concrete evidence accumulate before interpreting it. Useful for research summaries, incident reports, and analytical writing.

### Contrast driven

Organize the piece around a distinction that matters: reported vs normalized, trigger vs root cause, correlation vs causation, headline vs underlying reality, short-term vs long-term.

### Compressed reasoning

Remove steps the intended audience can infer. Do not turn every premise into a separate sentence. Useful when the original draft feels over-explained.

### Asymmetric structure

Give more space to the difficult, uncertain, or consequential point and less space to obvious background. Do not force equal-length sections or balanced lists when the ideas are not equally important.

## 4. Sentence-level principles

Natural prose is not created by random sentence lengths. Sentence shape should follow cognitive function.

Use shorter sentences for:

- a decisive claim;
- a correction;
- a consequence;
- a transition where emphasis matters.

Use longer sentences when several clauses belong to one logical unit and splitting them would make the reasoning feel artificial.

Prefer direct syntax, but do not make every sentence subject-verb-object. Use subordination when one idea genuinely depends on another.

Avoid habitual filler such as:

- “It is important to note that”;
- “In today’s rapidly evolving landscape”;
- “This highlights the importance of”;
- “Ultimately” when the conclusion is already obvious;
- repeated “Furthermore,” “Moreover,” “Additionally,” and similar signposts.

Do not ban any individual phrase mechanically. Remove it when it contributes no meaning.

## 5. Paragraph-level principles

Each paragraph should have a job. Common jobs include:

- establish a fact;
- advance a claim;
- explain a mechanism;
- qualify a claim;
- introduce evidence;
- compare alternatives;
- handle a limitation;
- draw an implication.

Two consecutive paragraphs should not perform the same job merely with different subject matter unless the structure requires it.

Paragraphs do not need equal length. A one-sentence paragraph can be appropriate when the sentence carries enough weight; a long paragraph can be appropriate when a chain of reasoning should remain intact.

## 6. Epistemic fidelity

Preserve uncertainty with particular care.

Examples:

- “may cause” is not “causes”;
- “was associated with” is not “produced”;
- “management estimates” is not an established fact;
- “the logs do not show” is not evidence that the event did not occur;
- “base case assumes” is not “will happen.”

When rewriting, models often make prose sound cleaner by quietly strengthening claims. Treat this as a factual error.

## 7. Human voice without fabrication

If the user provides writing samples, infer style from observable textual features:

- average sentence density;
- use of contractions;
- directness;
- vocabulary level;
- tolerance for fragments;
- use of rhetorical questions;
- humor or dryness;
- first-person frequency;
- degree of formality;
- preferred paragraph length.

Do not infer or invent life experience, identity, beliefs, or anecdotes solely to make the writing seem personal.

## 8. Revision sequence

A strong default sequence is:

1. preserve the content lock;
2. choose a structure;
3. rewrite the full document;
4. remove repeated rhetorical patterns;
5. tighten abstraction and filler;
6. compare every number, name, qualification, causal relationship, and conclusion against the source;
7. return only the final text unless an audit was requested.
