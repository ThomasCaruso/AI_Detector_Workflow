A product can be excellent and still fail if too few people encounter it. Early-stage founders often spend disproportionate time polishing features because product work feels concrete and controllable, while distribution feels uncertain. Product quality determines retention after acquisition, but acquisition has to happen first. Distribution also improves product development because more users create more behavioral evidence about what actually matters. The implication is not that marketing can rescue a bad product. It is that product development and distribution should operate as a feedback loop rather than sequential phases.
